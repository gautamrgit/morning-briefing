Four readings

1. Only two of six converted growth into margin

Nestlé and Colgate expanded. HUL contracted 40bps, Dabur merely held, PGHH went backwards on both lines. Same input environment, six outcomes — so cost programmes and mix, not commodities, decided this quarter. Colgate's "Funding the Growth" and Dabur's "Project Samriddhi" got explicit credit; HUL's margin defence came from ad spend instead.

2. Colgate is the cleanest reinvestment case

Gross margin up 110bps, EBITDA margin down ~150bps, ad spend up 33.7%. A company spending its cost savings on brand rather than banking them — and rewarded modestly. HUL did the inverse and was punished, then forgiven.

3. PGHH is the one genuine negative — with three caveats

  • Distorted base. Q1 FY26's ₹192 cr PAT was itself flagged as inflated by lower advertising versus a heavy-spend base period. Two-year stacked, the picture is flatter than dramatic.
  • FY26 was already flat. Full-year revenue flat, operating profit +18%, margin 27.3% — achieved partly through reduced advertising. That's the HUL playbook run for a full year; Q1 FY27 is arguably the bill arriving.
  • Vicks is monsoon-dependent. Healthcare demand tracks respiratory illness, and this quarter had a delayed monsoon.

Still, PGHH raised ad spend 21% while revenue fell 5% — the reverse sequencing of everyone else. Colgate and Nestlé spent from strength; PGHH is spending into weakness, which reads as share defence. Pre-results notes flagged exactly that: analyst concern over Whisper market share and promotional intensity in feminine hygiene.

4. Ad spend direction beats level

Colgate at 15.8% of sales versus Dabur at 6.1% is a category difference — oral care is a two-player share war, ayurvedic staples are distribution-led. Direction is what matters. HUL's ratio is falling; PGHH's is rising for the wrong reason.

Read-across for Reckitt: the −50bps gross margin sits between HUL and the expanders, and the Fuel for Growth framing — savings creating "greater capacity to invest" — puts it rhetorically in the Colgate camp. Whether H2 bears that out is the thing to watch.
The context that improved overnight: Reckitt India's high-single-digit growth, led by Dettol and Harpic, looks stronger set against a quarter in which a direct multinational competitor in adjacent hygiene categories went backwards — and against P&G's flat global organic growth.

If GCPL shows the same Home Care pattern HUL did — strong revenue, flat segment profit — that confirms aisle-wide promotional intensity rather than an HUL-specific execution issue. If GCPL converts, the problem was HUL's.
