## 🧘 Philosophy

**On the quarter that grew and shrank at the same time** — HUL sold more than it has in three years and still saw its stock fall 6%, because volume growth and margin compression arrived in the same set of numbers. The Stoics were insistent that a thing be judged by what it actually is rather than by the single dimension we happen to be watching — and a business, like a life, is never adequately described by one metric moving in one direction. The discipline for tomorrow's Reckitt print is the same one HUL's investors failed at today: hold both facts at once, rather than letting whichever number moved most violently write the whole story.

**On the difference between the price and the disruption** — Brent fell 11% in a day on news of a pause, yet fewer than ten ships crossed the Strait of Hormuz — a tenth of normal traffic. The market repriced the fear; the physical constraint barely moved. Heraclitus argued that appearance and underlying reality routinely diverge, and that most error comes from mistaking the visible signal for the thing itself. Anyone planning supply chains or input costs off the oil price alone this week is reading the sentiment, not the shipping lane — and those two will reconverge eventually, but not necessarily in the direction the price is currently suggesting.
