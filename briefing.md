## 🧘 Philosophy

**On the guidance cut that didn't happen** — Jefferies upgraded Reckitt to Buy last week partly on the expectation of a formal guidance cut it framed as a healthy "clearing event." The cut never came; guidance held, Q2 accelerated to 4.7%, and the stock rose 4.6% anyway. Epictetus insisted that the thing which disturbs us is rarely the event itself but our judgement about it in advance — and here the market had priced a disappointment that management simply declined to deliver. The lesson isn't that pessimistic forecasts are wrong, but that a conclusion held confidently before the evidence arrives is a liability regardless of which direction it points.

**On the same number read twice** — HUL fell 6% on Tuesday and rose 4.93% on Wednesday, on identical information. Nothing about the quarter changed overnight; only the frame did. Heraclitus held that opposites can be true of the same thing depending on the vantage point, and that most disputes are really about position rather than fact. It's a useful corrective to the instinct to treat day-one market reaction as the verdict on a result — the market's first read is a reflex, its second read is an opinion, and only the third or fourth is worth building a plan around.
