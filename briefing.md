## 🧘 Philosophy

**On distinguishing the transitory from the structural** — Jefferies' whole upgrade case rests on separating Reckitt's genuinely one-off setbacks — sanctions, a regulatory shift, a bad flu season — from anything indicating deeper decline, and concluding the remaining 80% of the business grew at 5%. The Stoics made much the same move in reverse: examining a misfortune closely enough to see which part is genuinely within your control and which is simply circumstance. It's a discipline that cuts both ways — the same clear-eyed sorting that stops a bad quarter being over-read should also stop a good one being over-credited.

**On the value of intervention before the crisis** — The sequoia study found that trees in stands treated with prescribed burns survived extreme wildfires that killed thousands of untreated neighbours, some of which had stood for millennia. Ancient longevity offered no protection; deliberate, uncomfortable preparation did. Aristotle argued that practical wisdom shows itself in acting well in advance of necessity rather than responding skilfully once forced. Whether the fire is literal, commercial or geopolitical, the pattern holds: the costly, unglamorous work done before the pressure arrives is usually what determines who's still standing after.
