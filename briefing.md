## 🧬 AI & Health

**Mayo's dermoscopy AI scores lesions — and shows its reasoning** — DEXI (Dermoscopy Explainable Intelligence) assigns each skin lesion a 0–10 risk score and flags which image features drove it: asymmetry, border irregularity, colour variation. At a 3.0 threshold it caught 93.4% of cancerous lesions and correctly cleared 75% of benign ones, on a retrospective set of 261 lesions from 178 patients, trained on 66,000+ annotated images. The explainability is the commercially interesting part — a score a clinician can interrogate clears adoption hurdles a black box does not. It does not account for age, medication or melanoma history, and underperforms on severely atypical nevi. *([Mayo Clinic Proceedings via The Outpost](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/mayo-clinic-s-ai-tool-scores-skin-lesions-for-cancer-risk-with-93-4-accuracy-32561/) · 22 Oct 2026)*

**Hospital staff say the governance for medical AI does not exist yet** — Interviews with hospital staff, analysed thematically and cross-checked with topic modelling, surfaced consistent gaps in accountability, informed consent and oversight of medical AI. The authors propose embedding ethical requirements across the full lifecycle rather than retrofitting after incidents, plus a multi-actor risk-based review process. They are explicit that the framework has not been implemented or evaluated. The useful read is that the constraint on clinical AI is now institutional rather than technical. *([BMC Medical Ethics](https://bioengineer.org/hospital-staff-reveal-ethical-dilemmas-of-ai-in-medicine-and-propose-governance-paths/) · 27 Oct 2026)*

## 🌿 Nature & Science

**Gut bacteria account for about 15% of the variation in how fast people age** — A University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa team analysed stool and blood from 123 adults across a wide age range, measuring biological ageing with DunedinPACE, a DNA-methylation pace-of-ageing marker. *Bifidobacterium adolescentis* was among the species most tied to slower ageing, *Succinivibrio dextrinosolvens* among those tied to faster. The authors are explicit that this is correlational and establishes no cause and effect — the 15% is a share of variance, not a lever anyone has pulled yet. *([Scientific Reports via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261026225620.htm) · 27 Oct 2026)*

**Some brains resist Alzheimer's — and it may be about how cells behave, not how many there are** — Netherlands Institute for Neuroscience researchers compared immature hippocampal neurons across people with no pathology, people with Alzheimer's dementia, and cognitively resilient people carrying Alzheimer's brain changes. The groups differed less in cell counts than in cell behaviour: in resilient individuals the cells showed survival and damage-coping programs active, with lower inflammation and cell-death signalling. Senior author Evgenia Salta describes the function as "fertilizer" for a failing garden. Inferred from tissue data, not directly tested. *([Cell Stem Cell via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261025222538.htm) · 27 Oct 2026)*

**"D-rex" — a 20-inch fungus, and a whole new family** — Kunming Institute of Botany researchers described *Dianjunus rex* from Yunnan, with reproductive structures up to 50cm tall and 2.2kg, the largest known in the phylum Ascomycota. It grows from the woody roots of Chinese cork oak, and genetic analysis placed it in a new genus and a new family, Dianjunaceae, with a lineage emerging roughly 65 million years ago — about a million years after the dinosaur extinction, when dead plant material was suddenly abundant. The finding is mostly a reminder of how much of China's forest fungal diversity is still undescribed. *([Frontiers in Fungal Biology via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261025073034.htm) · 26 Oct 2026)*

## 🧘 Philosophy

**The difference between a constraint and a failure**

Reckitt's Q3 miss came with an unusual shape: consumers wanted more Lysol than the factories could make. Demand was there, sell-out grew, and the revenue did not. That is a constraint, not a failure of the thing the business exists to do — and the market still took 7.1% off, because a market prices outcomes and cannot easily price the counterfactual. There is something worth sitting with here about how organisations learn. A quarter lost to weak demand teaches you about your brand; a quarter lost to a bottleneck teaches you about your system. They feel identical from the outside and demand opposite responses from the inside. The discipline is refusing to let the external verdict, which cannot tell them apart, decide which lesson you take.

**On growth you did not choose**

Core volume grew 0.1% and price/mix 3.2%. In the usual framing that is low-quality growth — buying the topline rather than earning it. But the pricing was taken to offset input costs tied to a conflict thousands of miles away, which makes it less a strategic choice than a forced move. Aristotle drew the line at voluntariness: an act done under compulsion sits differently in the moral ledger than one freely chosen, even when the act looks the same. The same holds commercially. The honest question is not whether price-led growth is bad, but how much of yours was actually a decision — because only the chosen part is something you can get better at.

**Fifteen per cent, and the temptation of a number**

The Hawaiʻi gut-ageing study found that bacterial differences accounted for about 15% of the variation in biological ageing pace, and the authors went out of their way to say it establishes no cause and effect. That caveat will not survive contact with the supplements aisle. It is the oldest move in commercial science communication: take a share of variance from a correlational study of 123 people and sell it as a mechanism you can buy. Hume's point about causation was that we never observe it directly — we observe sequence and constant conjunction, and supply the causal link ourselves out of habit. Marketing lives in exactly that gap. Which is a reason to be careful on the way out as well as sceptical on the way in: the gap is where credibility is built and where it is spent.
